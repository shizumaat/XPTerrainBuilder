## 1. Attribution (measured on the owner's own plans)

Instrument: `tools/v2_rebake_replay.py plan` on the owner's rebake plans and
meshes (KLAX 1.0.409 of 2026-10-20; LFPG 2026-10-24 as copied by lane
lpackcut; ELLX 2026-10-24), with a lane probe wrapped round
`airside_deck_seat.airside_zero` recording every deck it calls class A.

Class A's single derivation site is `airside_deck_seat.airside_zero`. Its
path 2 asks only that ANY ring vertex of the deck stand on an AIRSIDE role
(`law.tables.role_side`, which includes the graded strip and pads). KLAX's
`Misc/People_Mover/Tracks.obj` (one FLAG deck, ring 3.17 km x 0.45 km, deck
top +19.35) passes because 1 of its 80 ring vertices is on an airside face;
`airside_end_ground` then splits its airside edge stations into "two ends"
by 2-means even though the airport's pavement meets the deck at ONE place
(4 of 1,490 edge stations, one contiguous stretch). Seated flush at those
"end lines" (36.29 / 31.20), its zero is 14.39: the elevated track lowered
about 16 m.

Why not the footprint's own pavement: the deck interior is a HOLE in every
source. apt.dat pavement covers 0.00 of two of the three LFPG `H2.obj`
decks and of `H16.obj` (the pack leaves the bridge to its deck object); the
graded faces under the LFPG decks are mostly none at all (217 of 295 cells
on H2). So the witness is where the pavement meets the deck's edge.

## 2. Rule (implemented, `airside_deck_seat.pavement_runs_across`)

On the stations `airside_end_ground` already reads (every
`abutment_sample_step_m` along the ring edge, the role one step OUTSIDE it):

1. a station is a PAVEMENT station when its role is the airport's own
   taxiway, apron or runway pavement — the rolled-on roles
   (`airside_group_seat.airside_pavement`, `law.tables.rolled_on_roles`);
   the graded strip, pads and roads are not;
2. a CONTACT is a maximal run of pavement stations along the edge;
3. the pavement RUNS ACROSS the deck when two separate contacts face each
   other across it: their mean outward normals oppose (dot < 0), each
   contact lies on the inward side of the other, and the midpoint of the
   chord between their centroids lies inside the deck's ring.
4. one contact (a touch at one end), no contact, and a ring wholly
   surrounded by pavement (nothing passes under it: not a bridge) are not.

No number is chosen: the step is the law's `abutment_sample_step_m`, the
tests are signs and point-in-polygon. A deck refused is counted
`airside_deck_touch_only` and is seated as any other deck (10-22m / 26d /
22c), not by class A.

## 5. A refused bridge frame keeps one zero (round 2, master's order)

Round 1 refused class A for ELLX `Bridge_2` and its 9 bodies spread from
355.58 to 369.61. Why no existing pass held the frame together:
`rigid_open` (10-22c) asks `open_frame`, which needs EVERY member to be a
deck kind; three of the four members (.001, .002, .004: the supports and a
tunnel wall) are not. It also works per placement only, and the frame is
four placements. `group_zero` (22o) finds no §16g root for any of the 9
bodies. The only rule that moved the whole frame was class A's own: "every
body of its frame moves with it when the frame IS the bridge".

Kept, not added: a frame class A refuses only for 27c is still a bridge
frame when it IS the bridge (the same §16e (3) footprint test,
`airside_deck_seat._is_the_bridge`, shared with `airside_frame`), and a
bridge moves as one (10-22c/f), deck first (10-22d).
`airside_deck_seat.refused_bridge` names the deck. The placement stage cuts
that deck first (it carries; it depends on nothing) and takes 10-22c's own
zero of its bodies (`open_frame_seat.open_zero`, which `rigid_open` now
shares: the datum body's zero, else the lowest). Every body of every member
is then re-anchored to it (`open_frame_seat.frame_zero`); counted as
`refused_bridge_frames`.
