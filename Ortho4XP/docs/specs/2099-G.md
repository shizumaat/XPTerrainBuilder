## 1. The rule (general)

G1 THE WALLED RAMP. A mouth group's wing cut (`planar/wing_cut`, `WingCut.lines`) lines a
ramp road: the mouth's road (`_mouth_roads`) continued along its OSM ways through the cut
region. Along it the road stands on the foot of the wall line it faces
(`model/wing_cut.wing_floor_y`, already the wing-cut rows), climbing at no more than
`[tunnel] ramp_max_grade`, to WHERE ITS WALLS END: the station of the last wall line
station abeam the road (the largest route distance s of a station whose `_facing` reads
the road).

G2 MAIN ROAD AND SPUR. At the walls' end, the OSM ways meeting the ramp road within one
lane width of that station are read: the MAIN road is the way that continues the tunnel
road (the ramp road's own way, else the way with the least turn); a SPUR is a way leaving
it there whose far end lies on stage-1 airside within `spur_max_m` (owner: "short").

G3 THE SPUR'S CLIMB. The spur climbs from the main road's level at its junction to the
airside it meets, at no more than the road cap (`service_road` longitudinal 0.10), over its
FREE RUN: the length of the spur outside the main road's ribbon and outside airside
pavement. The main road stays on the foot (G1) to the junction.

G4 FEASIBILITY (pre-registered; the brief's "if the spur can't make it, report"). Let
z_c be the airside level where the spur meets it, z_f the foot at the walls' end, d the
route distance walls' end -> junction, r the spur's free run. The rule is applied only if

    z_c - (z_f + ramp_max_grade * d) <= road_cap * r     and
    the main road shares no edge with that airside between the walls' end and the junction

(a shared edge holds the main road's own edge at the airside level: airside is king,
the road's transverse cap 2 %). Otherwise NOTHING changes and the case is reported.
