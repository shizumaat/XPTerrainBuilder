## §1 THE BASE PROFILE (derived ONCE, at the pack read)

**(1) Definitions, in the authored frame of one member** (`ObjGeometry`, `airport/obj8.py:72`; the solid triangles with their vertices; draped triangles never count). A **HORIZONTAL FACE** has |n_y| ≥ `[base_profile] horizontal_ny` (0.95). A **PLANE** is a height cluster of horizontal faces (0.25 m bins, merged while adjacent bins are within `[placement] split_tol_m` 0.3) with face area ≥ `[building_pad] min_area_m2` (250, `structures.toml:494`) — below that it is furniture. Its **POLYGON** is the plan UNION of its faces (shapely, as `_plan_footprint`, `obj8.py:861`), never the bbox and never the hull (an L-shaped lot around a building would otherwise "contain" the building). A plane is a **ROOF / DECK / MEZZANINE** when the solid vertices of the UNIT (§1 (3)) lying ≥ `[basin] contact_band_m` (1.0, `structures.toml:588`) below it strictly inside its polygon (eroded by `identity.min_distinct_spacing_m` 0.5) are DISTRIBUTED under it — their convex hull covers ≥ `[base_profile] roof_support_fraction` (0.25) of the polygon's area (the shelter roof's column feet: 5,954 vertices inside, hull **156 %** of the 6,149 m² roof; a mezzanine over its floor; a bridge deck over its piers, deck law's anyway). Otherwise it is a **BASE PLANE**, and the cells within `[seam] pad_frontage_m` (3.0) of those few lower vertices are TRIMMED off it to the lower plane (site 2's lot: 157 lower vertices of the unit inside, hull **0.2 %** of the 6,394 m² lot — the garage threshold where FS_1/FS_3 feet meet it — trimming 31 m², 0.5 %). Measured 2026-10-01 on the KASE pack; A1's roof fixture is the twin. The **FEET** are the ground-contact vertices of §17 (B) (within `contact_band_m` of the member's lowest vertex), unchanged. A **RISER** is the height difference between two adjacent base planes whose polygons are within `[seam] pad_frontage_m` (3.0) of each other; a riser under `[terrace] pad_terrace_floor_m` (1.0, 30i) WELDS (the two planes are one plane at the area-weighted height — a kerb is not a terrace).

**(2) The base profile of a member** is one of: **FLAT** — exactly one base plane, or none but feet whose plane fit over the contact set has grade ≤ `emit.within_shape.pad_slope_max` (1 %, `emit.toml:80`); **STEPPED** — ≥ 2 base planes after welding (site 2: 0 / +3.9 / banks); **SLOPED** — no base plane ≥ 250 m² but a contact set ≥ `[base_profile] sloped_min_extent_m` (20 m) whose plane fit has grade in (`pad_slope_max`, `[base_profile] sloped_max`] (8 %, the hard ceiling) with rms residual ≤ `split_tol_m`; **FEET** otherwise (a column-only shelter: site 1). A bank sheet steeper than `sloped_max` (site 2's 17 % staircase) is RISER geometry, not a plane: its cells join the riser between the planes it connects. Orientation is carried by the polygons (the frame affine `placement_affine`, `obj8.py:297`, with the DSF heading), so a pad's edges follow the base polygon.

**(3) The unit profile.** Members of one §16g unit are composed into ONE frame through the §16c contact graph the plan already carries (`rebake.json` `contacts`: welded part pairs give the members' relative offset exactly; `cluster_pads[].pad_offset_spread` already publishes it — 13.3 m at `unit:108`). The base/roof test of (1) runs on the COMPOSED unit (the HECA T3 upper floors whose walls live in sibling members read as roofs only here — §0 fact 10 is the per-member upper bound). The **ORIGIN PLANE** `p0` is the base plane whose polygon contains the unit's seat point (the §16g datum sample centre, `plan_unit_datums`, `footprint_unit.py:670`), else the largest base plane; every other plane carries its offset `Δy_k = y_k − y_0`. A unit with no base plane keeps today's law exactly.

**(4) ONE derivation site.** `airport/obj8_grade.base_profile(geom, comps)` beside `planes` (`obj8_grade.py:89`), called from the pack read inside `ResourceCache` (`obj8.py:317`) once per resource (the triangles and `solid_components` are already in memory there — this is one more O(triangles) pass; §5 bounds it), composed per unit in `airport/placement_family.plan_clusters` (the same function the cluster pad takes, `planar/cluster.py` module docstring: "one law, two readers"), published on the plan as `members[].base_profile` {verdict, planes:[{y, area_m2, polygon}], risers:[{a, b, dy}], slope:{grad_x, grad_z}} and on the sidecar (`platforms[].base_planes`, `cluster_pads[].plane_pads`). The planar stage reads it from `Airport.clusters` as it reads the cluster today; nothing re-derives it from the DSF or the OBJ.

## §2 THE PADS

**(1) Stepped base → one pad per base plane.** Where today the unit mints ONE `building` pad over its footprint union (`cluster.py`, §30 (4)), it mints one pad REGION per base plane, at the plane's polygon (clipped to the unit footprint union; the riser strip of (3) between neighbours), ref `<ref>/p<k>` (the block grammar `<ref>/b<k>` extended at its ONE site, `model/planar.py`, v1 C3 / flat-pad §3). Each plane pad is a §20/§30 pad in its own right: a platform + collar when ≥ `cluster_pad_min_m2` (`platform_split`, `planar/platform.py:134`), a conforming pad otherwise, the rows the pad law already mints (`platform_plane_rows :253`, `pad_flats`, `pads.py:664`). **The level law:** `p0` takes the unit's datum exactly as today (`plan_unit_datums` median / `hold_interval` `D_b`, flat-pad v2 §2 — the airside frontage decides it, airside is king); plane `k` is **PINNED TO `p0`**: ONE hard `Diff` row `z_{D_k} − z_{D_0} = Δy_k` between the two datum columns (`model/constraints.py`, the Band precedent at width 0), head `rulesets.base_profile.plane_offset` in `[design] hard_rulings` (`emit.toml:792`). No new column class, no new row generator: the plane pad's datum column is the platform's datum column. Q-111b blocks (`pad_blocks.plan_blocks :463`) run INSIDE one plane pad, never across a riser (a riser is already the cut).

**(2) Sloped base → one sloped pad.** The pad's plane rows (`platform_plane_rows`) carry the base's gradient `(g_x, g_z)` rotated by the heading as a FIXED gradient instead of the free tilt; `pad_slope_max` is lifted for THAT pad to its base grade + `split_tol_m / extent` (the object's own tolerance), bounded by `sloped_max`; `verify/pads.plane_residual` (`verify/pads.py:68`) reads the residual against the carried gradient. The seat datum is the pad's value at the seat point (the median rule 17t over a tilted plane is the plane at the median vertex — the lane posts both and uses the seat point).

**(3) The riser is a DECLARED terrace of the object's own height.** Between adjacent plane pads a strip of width `identity.min_distinct_spacing_m` (0.5 m; the two rims are two vertex rows 0.5 m apart along the riser line, never coincident XY — the identity law) is minted as the terrace, published as a `terrace_joints` record of **kind `base_step`** (beside `pad_terrace`, `publication.pad_terrace_joints :777`) with `declared_step_m = Δy`. The census reads it through the EXISTING step exemption: the pair is `building|building` → `building_to_building` (`check_grade.py:10083`); the exemption REGISTER gains one entry `base_plane_step` (`STEP_EXEMPTIONS`, `:10050`, the ruling 10-01f) so `terrace_actual_step` (`families.toml:103`) prices the EMITTED step against the DECLARED riser — one family extended, never a parallel rule. No 1:3 bank (`bank_slope`, `emit.toml:734`) under a building: the object's own riser faces cover the strip; the strip's triangles are the steep triangles of the wall-gap precedent (`emit/s2_adapter.py:21`, RULINGS 2026-09-01c), which is §4's emittability. Where a plane pad other than `p0` touches AIRSIDE (a stepped terminal fronting the apron at two levels), its frontage is a pad|apron terrace by 28b (`planar/pad_terrace.py`, floor 1.0 m) unless the owner rules otherwise (Q3).

**(4) Flat base longer than its pad can hold (site 1) is NOT a base-profile case.** `Shelters.OBJ` has one base height. The design that fits it is Q-111b's block cut (30r) extended from PLATFORM pads to CONFORMING pads (`platform_split` returns before `plan_blocks` for a pad under `cluster_pad_min_m2` or fronting no airside — site 1's rows are 6,774 m², inside an apron): cut chords at the object's own COMPONENT boundaries (the 24 bays are 217 welded components; a chord crossing 0 m of parts exists every ~10 m), block count = the smallest `k` whose blocks each span ≤ `frontage_hold_margin_m / grade` (0.3 / 1.36 % = 22 m → 11 blocks) OR, under an owner riser allowance `[base_profile] flat_block_riser_m` (Q1), fewer: with 1.0 m risers 247 m × 1.36 % = 3.4 m → **4 blocks**, each end within ±0.5 m. The §16c weld across a chord is severed by `pad_block_seat.split_units :277` as for any block, so the ONE rigid body becomes `k` bodies seated flat. This is pre-registered as the site-1 mechanism; the owner's number (Q1) decides `k`.

## §4 EMITTABILITY IN A HEIGHTFIELD MESH

A heightfield cannot carry a vertical face (`s2_adapter.py:21`, RULINGS 2026-09-01c). Two pads at `D_0` and `D_0 + Δy` need two vertex rows at DIFFERENT XY: the riser strip of §2 (3) is the identity spacing (0.5 m) wide, so a 3.9 m riser is a 0.5 m-run strip at ~780 % — the steepest cell the mesh admits, the wall-gap form, and the object's own riser faces (site 2: the +4.1..+6.6 bank sheets, 1,000 m²) stand on it. `merge_sub_spacing` sees identity spacing by construction. A sloped pad is one plane with one z per vertex. At the riser's ENDS the strip meets the unit's outer rim: where both plane pads are groundside the collar banks of both carry the ends; where the lower plane fronts airside the upper plane's end is cut back by `pad_frontage_m` as 28b cuts a touching apron (C5).
