## §1 THE MODEL

1. **A BUILDING PAD IS FLAT AT ONE DATUM = THE RIGID UNIT'S SEAT.** A platform block (§2's partition; ONE block where the unit passes the test) is ONE plane with tilt **0** at datum `D_b` (the cap-0 flat target is restored for a HELD block; 29s (A)'s released tilt applies only to a block that is NOT held, §2 (6)); the object stage seats the unit's bodies on `D_b` (§3 rows C16/C15). The 1 % ceiling still bounds a non-held block.
2. **THE FRONTAGE IS HELD AT THE DATUM — THE PAD LEADS, THE APRON FOLLOWS.** Every WELDED contact of the block (a collar outer vertex that is an airside vertex — `platform_contacts`, the set 29s already reads) takes a HARD two-sided row `z_q − D_b = 0` (`hard_tol_m` 0.02) **in stage 1**. `D_b` enters stage 1 as a FRONTAGE DATUM COLUMN — one virtual column per held block, an unknown of the airside problem like a vertex but with no position, registered by `airside_stage_vertices`; it is not a groundside vertex, so §20b's rule (no groundside column in stage 1) is kept, and 08k (4)'s bar on re-solve passes is kept (no pre-pass, no second stage 1). Stage 1 therefore solves the airside WITH the flat frontage as a law, and the solver — not a lag, not a one-way row — redistributes the relief. After stage 1, `D_b` is a constant; stage 2's platform plane rows (`platform_plane_rows`, hard) and one level row against the (now constant) contacts put the platform at `D_b` exactly. This is the reverse of #67 AT FRONTAGES ONLY: every other pad row (`pad_slope_max ceiling`, `frontage_level`, `groundside_frontage`, the collar bank) stays conforming, exactly where 29d left it.
3. **THE RELIEF IS REDISTRIBUTED, EACH FAMILY WITHIN ITS CAP.** The apron along the frontage is flat at `D_b`; its body grades away from the frontage under the tiered apron law (06w: 1 % preferred, **1.5 % hard in every direction**, `common.roles.apron`), into the taxiways it meets; a taxiway re-grades along its route under its letter cap (ICAO `icao.taxi` longitudinal / transverse **1.5 %** for letters C–F — HECA is F, SPJC is D — 3 % / 2 % for A–B; FAA 1.5 %) and its curvature law; the universal ceiling (29ac) binds over all; and the apron's preference rows (`apron.tiered_rows`) pay for it in the objective, reported per face as `apron_over_preference`. **Nothing here is a new row family: the caps that bound the redistribution are the hard rows stage 1 already carries.** The mesh model is a heightfield either way (30l corollary (b)): a held frontage is a set of vertex values, emittable as today.
4. **THE RUNWAY AND ITS ZONE BANDS NEVER MOVE.** The runway family's rows are hard and senior (the profile chord, K, transverse, seam pins §38); the adjacent-ground bands (`zones.adjacent_ground`) are one-way from the runway. The hold must not reach them through a taxi chain: §2's test treats every runway-family vertex and every vertex inside a runway's zone-2 half width (`zones.toml`) as PINNED (τ = 0), so a block whose flat frontage would need runway or zone motion is judged infeasible and split, never solved against the runway. Acceptance measures it (§4: runway movers 0 vs the reference).
5. **WHAT IS HELD: WELDED contacts only.** A facing frontage across bare ground (27a (9), `pad_fronting.facing`, 50 m reach) is not held: the ground between is a terrace by 27a (9) and the pad already takes the facing edge's level through `pad_fronting_level`; that stays (owner Q3 below if the owner wants facing frontages held too). A refused platform (`under_min_area` / `eroded_away`, 29s (E)) keeps its contact-led plate: it is not a unit. A courtyard (islands rule, 29d (b)) follows its pad and is flat at `D_b` with it.

## §2 THE FEASIBILITY TEST AT MINT TIME, AND THE SPLIT

The test runs in `planar/platform.platform_split` (the single derivation site of the platform, §3 C1) — after `apron_cut_to_pads` (23a) and `pad_terrace_split` (28b), before pass A nodes the airside — on the one level that exists at the arrangement, the DEM (`rim_relief_m` already reads it there), with a margin for the DEM being an unreliable witness. It is a Lipschitz-extension feasibility: the apron is a surface `z` with `|∇z| ≤ a` (a = the apron hard cap 1.5 %), equal to `D_b` on the held frontage and to the taxiway edge levels `T(q)` where the apron meets a taxiway; the taxiway edge itself may move by `τ_q` along its route within the taxi cap `t`. Feasible iff a datum exists that every taxi edge vertex `q` bordering the apron can reach:

1. **THE INTERVAL.** For block `b` with welded contacts `F_b`: `I_b = ∩_q [T(q) − a·d_q − τ_q, T(q) + a·d_q + τ_q]` over every taxi-family edge vertex `q` of the apron bodies the block fronts, `d_q` = the geodesic distance inside the apron from `q` to the block's frontage (the straight distance where the apron is convex between them; the lane uses the arrangement's own apron polygon and `shapely` shortest path through it), `T(q)` = the DEM at `q` (the proxy for stage 1's value there), and `τ_q = max(0, t·s_q − u_q) − margin` where `s_q` is the route distance from `q` to its nearest PINNED vertex (§1 (4): runway-family contact, zone-2 band, a junction touching a runway) and `u_q` the grade the route already spends between them (`|T(q) − T(pin)|`); the taxi cap `t` is the letter cap read from `law.tables` (never a literal). `margin` = NEW `[building_pad] frontage_hold_margin_m` **0.3 m** (= the §17 body float bar, the smallest error the owner reads): the DEM proxy's allowance. A test with `τ ≡ 0` is the HELD-TAXI test; the full test is the MOVED-TAXI test. **The block passes iff `I_b ≠ ∅`**; `D_b` = the point of `I_b` nearest the block's contact median (the level the least apron moves for), reported as `feasible_interval` in the sidecar.
2. **THE CHAIN.** Consecutive blocks along one apron edge are joined by that edge: between block `b`'s and block `b+1`'s frontage the apron edge (the collar's outer ring under the eaten link, welded, 08k (2): no joint inside the apron) grades at ≤ `a` over the frontage gap `g_b` (the ring distance between the two contact sets), so `|D_b − D_{b+1}| ≤ a·g_b`. The partition is feasible iff forward–backward interval propagation over the chain (`I_b` intersected with `I_{b±1} ± a·g_b`) leaves every `I_b` non-empty. A step the gap cannot carry is NOT an apron terrace joint (08k (2), `terrace_joint_route` stays CRITICAL): the partition moves the boundary (§2 (4)).
3. **THE VERDICT.** ONE block for the whole unit where the unit's own interval is non-empty under the MOVED-TAXI test; otherwise the unit **SPLITS** (30f; 29s A2 / Q-111 (a)) into the fewest blocks whose chain is feasible. Splitting is by RIGID BLOCK, never through a body.
4. **HOW BLOCKS ARE CHOSEN.** (a) The atoms are the unit's §16c (7)–(8) rigid clusters (`rigid_reach_m` 2.0, `placement_cut.comp_cluster`) of FOOTED members, read from the rebake plan the pad stage already has (`cluster_pad.cluster_polys` reads the same plan). (b) Clusters joined by a pack CONTACT (the 17s/17x ε-contact graph, `obj8_split_report --contact-pairs`'s population) are ONE atom: **a contact pair must never step** (17x's bar, ≤ 0.5 m, held UNCHANGED — pairs stepping today stay as they are, no new pair). (c) Atoms are ordered along the frontage (projection onto the apron edge's arc length) and grouped GREEDILY from the low end: a block grows atom by atom while its chain-propagated interval stays non-empty; the boundary is placed in the CLEAR SPACE between two atoms (never inside one), at the widest clear space within the last feasible atom's neighbourhood. (d) A block with no welded contact of its own (an atom behind the frontage) joins the block whose frontage it stands nearest and is seated on that block's `D_b` (its own ground residual reported, 17x (1)'s "member 1 m higher than its neighbour 600 m away is not a defect"). (e) More than **5** blocks for one unit is a STOP (report the chain, the intervals and the atoms; the owner decides — Q4).
5. **THE TERRACE BETWEEN BLOCKS** (28b's declared terrace, the pad|pad exemption `step_exemption_pad_to_pad`): each block is its own pad face pair (`<ref>/b<k>` platform + `<ref>/b<k>#collar`), the collar of block `b` and of block `b+1` meeting in the clear space between the atoms; the step `D_b − D_{b+1}` is a declared joint (`planar/shapes` joints, sidecar `terrace_joints` kind `pad_block`, published through `TOUCHING_STEPS` as 14x publishes touching clusters) carried as a 1:3 bank (`emit.design.bank_slope`) where the clear space ≥ `|ΔD| / bank_slope`, and as a RETAINING WALL (§47's class, the emitter's `retaining_wall` way, check_grade `_TERRACE_JOINT_WAY`) where it is narrower — a wall under a building is §31 (3) landside-visual, invisible from the cockpit, and mints no MOTION row. At the frontage the two blocks' apron edge grades between `D_b` and `D_{b+1}` over `g_b` (§2 (2)); the terrace never enters the apron.
6. **A ONE-BLOCK UNIT THAT FAILS THE TEST** (SPJC `building13`: one rigid body, no clear space to split at): it cannot split. DEFAULT (owner Q1): the block is FLAT at the datum that minimises the largest frontage miss (`D_b` = mid of the contacts' [min, max] clipped to what the taxi budget reaches), the hold rows for the reachable contacts are HARD and the unreachable contacts' rows are DROPPED to the pad-weight preference (they are the residual, priced but not law), so the apron comes as far as its caps allow and the rest is carried by the COLLAR under the building (the unit-platform spec §5 Q1 residual, `platform_rim_relief` per block); the wall foot along that stretch stands off the ground by the residual and every such body is REPORTED. Alternative for the owner: keep 29s's contact-led tilt for a one-body unit (today: ±1.9 m at `building13`'s ends).

## §4 WHAT THE AIRSIDE REFERENCE MEANS NOW (29p (1) amended by 30f)

29p (1) made the sw0929b airside the reference "any later build whose airside moves against it is measured against it first … a regression is a defect regardless of census counts"; 29w (6) re-cut it as `frames/reference/sw0929c_HECA.osm` (SPJC: `/tmp/harness/sw0929c_SPJC.osm`, the sweep patch, to be registered as `frames/reference/sw0929c_SPJC.osm` by the lane before its first arm). Under 30f the apron ALONG BUILDING FRONTAGES is ALLOWED to move — that is the ruling's content — and taxiways move only within their caps where a frontage pushes slope into them; the runway and its zone bands move 0. The bar `airside_value_delta` adjudicates on (C21), per airport:

1. **Runway family** (`runway`, shoulders, `graded_strip`, every zone-band vertex): movers > 0.01 m vs the reference = **0**. Not a tolerance: a runway mover is the hold reaching a pinned column, i.e. the §2 pin set missed a coupling — STOP and name the route.
2. **Taxi family**: every mover lies on a route that touches an apron body fronting a held block, its \|Δ\| ≤ the route's cap × the route distance from the mover to its nearest pinned vertex, and after the move every pair on that route is within its letter cap (the `taxi_longitudinal` / `transverse` / `taxi_box` families read the same counts as the reference or fewer). A taxi mover anywhere else is a defect. The count and worst are QUOTED per airport (expected: HECA tens of vertices near T2, SPJC near the terminal's two ends).
3. **Apron**: movers inside the apron bodies that front a held block are EXPECTED and quoted per block (count, worst, and the `apron_over_preference` delta); a mover in any other apron body is a defect (the hold rows reach only through the taxi chain, and (2) bounds that).
4. **Everything groundside** (pads, collars, lots, roads, strips beyond the zone bands) is not the airside reference (29w (1)) and is read by the census and the owner.

The reference frame is NOT re-cut by this round: a later build is still measured against sw0929c; what changes is the READING of an apron-frontage mover (expected) and a taxi mover (bounded), never of a runway mover (0).
